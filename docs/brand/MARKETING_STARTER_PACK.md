2. Try this: pick one string today and name every C, F, and G up to the 12th fret.

3. Chord tones are not abstract theory. They are the notes that make your chord sound like itself.

4. If you know the root, 3rd, and 5th, you are already reading the skeleton of the chord.

5. Scale degrees are movable. That is the magic. `1-3-5` means something in every key.

6. Five minutes of focused note recognition beats thirty minutes of wandering.

## Short-Form Content Pillars

### 1. Tiny Fretboard Wins

Quick exercises that viewers can try with or without the app.

Examples:

- Find every G on strings 6 and 1.
- Name the 3rd of C major, F major, and G major.
- Turn one chord shape into three note targets.

### 2. Theory You Can Touch

Explain one theory term by placing it on the guitar.

Examples:

- What is a chord tone?
- What does scale degree 5 do?
- Why roots are not enough.

### 3. Mistake Repair

Normalize common learner confusion and show a small fix.

Examples:

- "I know the shape, but not the notes."
- "I can play the scale, but not use it."
- "I forget everything past the 5th fret."

### 4. Product Loop

Show FretGarden doing one clear thing in under 20 seconds.

Examples:

- Start note drill, answer, get feedback.
- Miss a chord tone, review it, try again.
- Finish a short session and see weak spots.

## Short-Form Video Ideas
