# FretGarden Brand Strategy

## Brand Snapshot

FretGarden is a browser-based guitar fretboard practice app for learners who want the neck to feel less mysterious. The product helps players build fretboard fluency through short drills, immediate feedback, local progress, and lessons that connect directly to practice.

The first public message should stay focused on:

- Note recognition
- Chord tones
- Scale degrees

Advanced drills like interval landmarks, CAGED octave shapes, and triad inversions can remain available, but they should not carry the primary marketing promise yet.

## Positioning

FretGarden helps beginner and early-intermediate guitar players grow fretboard fluency through short, focused practice sessions for notes, chord tones, and scale degrees.

## Primary Promise

Know where you are on the guitar neck.

## Short Taglines

- Grow your fretboard fluency.
- Make the guitar neck familiar.
- Small drills. Stronger fretboard instincts.
- Practice notes, chord tones, and scale degrees.
- Learn the neck by doing.

Recommended starting tagline:

**Grow your fretboard fluency.**

It matches the FretGarden name, communicates progress, and avoids overpromising features the current app does not have.

## Audience

Primary:

- Beginner to early-intermediate guitarists
- Self-taught players who know basic chords but get lost beyond open shapes
- Learners who want structured, repeatable practice without a full course platform

Secondary:

- Guitar teachers who want a simple browser drill tool for students
- Returning players rebuilding theory and fretboard confidence

## What FretGarden Is

- A focused guitar fretboard practice app
- A low-friction drill tool
- A lesson-to-practice loop
- A way to turn note names, chord tones, and scale degrees into playable knowledge
- An early account experience for identity and future progress sync

## What FretGarden Is Not Yet

- A complete music school
- A video course platform
- An ear training app
- A tuner, recording tool, or backing-track app
- A cloud-progress, subscription, or social learning network

## Messaging Pillars

### 1. Fretboard Fluency

The app helps learners stop guessing and start recognizing where notes and musical relationships live on the neck.

Example copy:

> FretGarden turns the guitar neck into a place you can navigate, not a pattern you keep forgetting.

### 2. Short Practice Loops

The core habit is simple: open the app, run a quick drill, get feedback, and improve a little at a time.

Example copy:

> Five focused minutes can make the next practice session feel clearer.

### 3. Theory You Can Touch

Lessons should connect theory words to actual fretboard choices.

Example copy:

> Learn what a chord tone means, then find it under your fingers.

### 4. Calm Progress

FretGarden should feel encouraging, grounded, and practical. Avoid pressure, streak anxiety, or fake mastery claims.

Example copy:

> No leaderboard. No noise. Just a clearer map of the neck.

## Voice

FretGarden should sound:

- Warm
- Clear
- Practical
- Musically literate
- Encouraging without being hype-heavy

Avoid:

- Shaming players for not knowing theory
- Overclaiming fast results
- Dense academic theory language
- Vague creator-economy promises
- Calling the app an all-in-one platform before it is one

## Visual Identity

### Visual Direction

FretGarden should feel like a calm practice space rather than a neon guitar utility. The strongest visual metaphor is not a literal garden scene. It is the feeling of small daily growth: seeds, rows, roots, fret lines, note markers, and patterns becoming visible.

### Starter Palette

The current app already leans warm and earthy. Keep that direction:

- Garden canvas: `#f3eadb`
- Soft cream: `#fffaf1`
- Deep leaf: `#2f4537`
- Olive: `#3f5f4b`
- Sage: `#78966f`
- Clay: `#b86f45`
- Ochre: `#d6a34f`
- Ink: `#24231c`

### Logo Concept

The starter mark combines:

- Fretboard rows
- Note dots as seeds
- A small sprout suggesting growth

Use it as a direction to refine, not as a final locked logo.

### Logo Usage

Recommended naming usage:

- Product and logo: **FretGarden**
- In prose when sentence flow matters: **FretGarden**
- Domain/social handle if needed: `fretgardenapp`, `tryfretgarden`, or `playfretgarden`

Avoid:

- `Fret Garden` as the primary app name
- `fretGarden` camelCase in user-facing copy
- Overly botanical illustration that makes the product look like a meditation app

## Launch Story

FretGarden exists because a lot of guitarists can play shapes before they can name what they are playing. The app gives them a calmer way to connect the fretboard to music theory: one short drill, one lesson, one visible improvement at a time.

## Naming Due Diligence

Before public launch:

- Check domain availability across `.com`, `.app`, and practical modifiers like `tryfretgarden.com`.
- Search USPTO and common law usage for music education, apps, software, and guitar products.
- Check App Store and Google Play for confusingly similar names.
- Reserve social handles if the name remains the direction.

This document is not legal advice. Treat it as product and marketing direction until a trademark check is complete.
